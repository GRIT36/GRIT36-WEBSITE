Session 1 / Strategy formation

Six schools of strategy

The course distinguishes prescriptive schools, which explain how strategy should be formulated, from descriptive schools, which examine how strategy actually forms. Each emphasizes different actors, assumptions and sources of insight.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

SchoolCentral idea and actorImportant limitation
DesignAchieve a fit between internal strengths and the environment; the CEO is the principal designer.Separating thought from action can obstruct learning. An explicit design may reduce flexibility.
PlanningFormalize strategy through a structured process; planners support the CEO.Detailed analysis can become paralysis, and planning can become detached from action.
PositioningSelect a defensible competitive position through analysis of industry and generic strategies.Economic analysis may overlook politics, cognition and emerging opportunities.
EntrepreneurialStrategy develops around an entrepreneur's vision, intuition and experience.Direction can become heavily dependent on one leader's perspective.
CognitiveMental maps, bounded rationality and dominant logic shape strategic interpretation.Experience can become a filter that prevents managers from seeing change.
LearningStrategy emerges through experimentation, incremental action and collective learning.Learning requires interpreting evidence; repetition alone does not establish that a belief is correct.

Key distinctions

A framework for comparing frameworks

The six schools address different aspects of strategy. A positioning analysis may explain a market choice; a learning perspective may explain how the organization discovered it. The opening lecture explicitly rejects the idea that any one school captures all strategy formation.

Dominant logic

Managers develop shared mental maps through experience in a core business and may carry them into new businesses. These maps simplify complexity, but can also create inertia. Renewal may therefore require unlearning a previously successful formula.

How to use it

  1. Identify whether the question concerns strategic content, a decision process or organizational learning.
  2. Use one lens to develop an explanation and another to challenge it.
  3. State the assumptions that would make the chosen lens inappropriate.

Keep in mind

Prescriptive does not mean correct and descriptive does not mean unstructured. The distinction concerns the purpose of the theory.

Pause & recall

Why compare several schools?

Reveal the explanation

They highlight different mechanisms and expose one another's blind spots.

S01 / Foundations

The strategic management process

Strategy links long-term direction, choices and resource allocation. The course treats it as a connected process: strategic inputs inform formulation and implementation; outcomes and experience feed back into the next round of decisions.

Read the framework ↗S01 / Foundations

Industry position & resource-based advantage

The I/O model and the resource-based model explain above-average returns from different starting points. One begins with industry structure; the other begins with the firm's resources and capabilities. They provide complementary questions for diagnosing an opportunity.

Read the framework ↗S01 / Strategy formation

Deliberate & emergent strategy

Realized strategy can contain both deliberate choices and patterns that emerge through action. The lecture separates what was intended from what was implemented, abandoned or learned along the way.

Read the framework ↗S01 / Industry analysis

Porter's Five Forces

Five Forces examines how competition over economic value extends beyond direct rivals. The focal unit is an industry: suppliers, buyers, substitutes and potential entrants can all constrain its profit potential.

Read the framework ↗S01 / Positioning

Generic competitive strategies

Generic strategies combine the source of advantage with the breadth of the competitive target. The central choices are cost leadership, differentiation and focus; the slides also discuss integrated or hybrid positioning.

Read the framework ↗S01 / Positioning

Bowman's Strategy Clock

The Strategy Clock maps competitive positions using price and perceived value to the customer. It makes room for combinations such as low price with low added value, low price, hybrid value, differentiation and focused differentiation.
