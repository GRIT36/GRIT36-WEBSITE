Session 1 / Strategy formation

Deliberate & emergent strategy

Realized strategy can contain both deliberate choices and patterns that emerge through action. The lecture separates what was intended from what was implemented, abandoned or learned along the way.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

TermMeaningReview question
Intended strategyThe direction originally envisaged.What did we plan to do?
Deliberate strategyThe intended elements that were actually realized.Which commitments became action?
Unrealized strategyIntended elements that did not materialize.What was dropped or prevented, and why?
Emergent strategyA pattern that developed without being fully specified in the original intention.What repeated behavior or discovery created a new direction?
Realized strategyThe resulting pattern of action, including deliberate and emergent elements.What strategy did the organization actually enact?

Key distinctions

Autonomous and induced behavior

Burgelman's model distinguishes autonomous initiatives, often developed from below and supported through an internal political process, from induced initiatives developed within the existing strategy, structure and vision. Both can contribute to corporate entrepreneurship.

Learning is not retrospective decoration

A useful review preserves what was known at the time. If every successful outcome is described as a plan after the event, it becomes impossible to learn whether the result came from a sound hypothesis, adaptation or fortunate circumstances.

How to use it

  1. Record original objectives and key assumptions.
  2. Compare them with the actual sequence of decisions.
  3. Identify emerging patterns and decide which deserve explicit support.

Keep in mind

Emergence does not imply that objectives, discipline or accountability are unnecessary. It describes how patterns can form outside a fully specified plan.

Pause & recall

Is realized strategy the same as intended strategy?

Reveal the explanation

No. Some intentions remain unrealized, while new strategic patterns may emerge.

The strategic management process

Strategy links long-term direction, choices and resource allocation. The course treats it as a connected process: strategic inputs inform formulation and implementation; outcomes and experience feed back into the next round of decisions.

Industry position & resource-based advantage

The I/O model and the resource-based model explain above-average returns from different starting points. One begins with industry structure; the other begins with the firm's resources and capabilities. They provide complementary questions for diagnosing an opportunity.

Six schools of strategy

The course distinguishes prescriptive schools, which explain how strategy should be formulated, from descriptive schools, which examine how strategy actually forms. Each emphasizes different actors, assumptions and sources of insight.

Read the framework ↗S01 / Industry analysis

Porter's Five Forces

Five Forces examines how competition over economic value extends beyond direct rivals. The focal unit is an industry: suppliers, buyers, substitutes and potential entrants can all constrain its profit potential.

Generic competitive strategies

Generic strategies combine the source of advantage with the breadth of the competitive target. The central choices are cost leadership, differentiation and focus; the slides also discuss integrated or hybrid positioning.

Bowman's Strategy Clock

The Strategy Clock maps competitive positions using price and perceived value to the customer. It makes room for combinations such as low price with low added value, low price, hybrid value, differentiation and focused differentiation.
