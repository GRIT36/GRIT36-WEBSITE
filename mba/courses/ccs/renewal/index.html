Session 2 / Business model innovation

Replication, renewal & dual focus

Business model replication develops and extends an existing logic; business model renewal changes the model more substantially. The lecture distinguishes fixation, a focus on one route and dual focus on both routes.

Key distinctions

Performance evidence: keep the comparison intact

The lecture's performance chart uses fixation as the reference point. It reports differences of 8% for focus on renewal, 13% for focus on replication and 18% for dual focus. These are relative performance differences in that slide, not profit margins, success probabilities or guaranteed effects for a particular firm. The slide does not provide enough methodology to turn those figures into a universal rule.

Context changes the result

The following diagrams distinguish the performance patterns of replication and renewal and show that competitiveness in the environment changes the relationship. The right choice therefore depends on the context and on the extent of innovation, not just the attractiveness of the word 'renewal'.

Managing dual focus

A company pursuing both routes must make resource and organizational choices visible. The course's practical guidance explicitly calls for managing conflicts between business models rather than hiding them.

How to use it

  1. Describe which parts of the existing model remain stable.
  2. Identify whether proposed changes improve that logic or replace important relationships.
  3. Assess environmental dynamism and competitive pressure.
  4. Specify how competing resource claims will be resolved.

Keep in mind

Replication and renewal are analytical distinctions, not moral rankings. More innovation does not imply better performance at every level.

Pause & recall

What does '18%' mean on the course chart?

Reveal the explanation

The reported performance difference for dual focus relative to the chart's reference group, not a forecast return.
