Session 5 / Alliances

Alliance forms & governance

Alliances coordinate activities across firm boundaries. The lecture distinguishes equity-based arrangements from contractual arrangements, alongside other associational forms, and explains how the form affects commitment and coordination.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

FormCore arrangementWhat to distinguish
Equity joint venturePartners establish a separate jointly owned entity.The entity coexists with its owners; ownership, governance, risks and returns are shared.
CooperativeMembers jointly own and run an organization providing services to them.The course emphasizes democratic control and benefits linked to patronage rather than a conventional JV's shareholding logic.
Minority participation / cross-shareholdingFirms take non-controlling equity positions.A relationship can be established without creating a new entity.
Vertical contractual allianceA long-term arrangement across different supply-chain stages.Supplier–buyer or distribution relationships with intended joint benefits.
Horizontal contractual allianceCooperation at the same or similar value-chain stage.Joint R&D, production, distribution, marketing or branding.
LicensingAuthorized use of specified assets or intellectual property for negotiated compensation.Define the field of use, exclusivity, duration and payment structure.
FranchisingA broader license to use a business formula.Includes an operating approach and often continuing support, training and controls.

Key distinctions

Licensing versus franchising

In the lecture, all franchising arrangements are a form of licensing, but not all licensing includes a franchise. A franchise normally coordinates a broader set of practices and identity elements, so the relationship involves more than permission to use one asset.

Alliance or acquisition?

The decision framework compares the nature of synergies, the resources involved, redundant resources, market uncertainty and competition for resources. It associates different combinations with nonequity alliances, equity alliances or acquisitions. Use the original decision table below to examine the dimensions together rather than choosing from one isolated factor.

How to use it

  1. Define the joint objective and the resources each party contributes.
  2. Compare the required commitment, control and coordination.
  3. Choose a form consistent with those requirements and plan how the relationship will evolve.

Keep in mind

The classifications are the course's conceptual descriptions. A particular agreement's consequences depend on its actual design and context.

Pause & recall

Must an alliance create a new company?

Reveal the explanation

No. Contractual alliances and direct equity participation can coordinate firms without forming a new entity.

VRINO examines whether a resource, routine, capability or competence can support sustained competitive advantage. The course uses five tests: Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, Non-substitutable and Organized.

PESTEL organizes changes in the wider environment into six categories. Its purpose in the course's analysis sequence is to identify relevant external developments before examining industry structure, competitors and customers.

Industry structure, life cycle & strategic groups

The course separates three levels of industry analysis: industry characteristics, strategic groups and rivalry between specific competitors. These levels answer different questions and should not be collapsed into one competitor list.

The four corners of competitor analysis

To reason about a competitor's future strategy, examine its drivers, management assumptions, current strategy and capabilities. The course places these four perspectives around the question of what the competitor is likely to do next.

SWOT as a synthesis of analysis

In the course sequence, SWOT is the result of internal and external analysis. Strengths and weaknesses concern the organization; opportunities and threats concern the environment. The useful work lies in explaining how these factors interact.

Ecosystems, complements & structure

An ecosystem links multiple actors through complementary contributions to a value proposition without placing them all under one hierarchy. The analysis asks how joint value comes about and which interdependencies must be aligned.
