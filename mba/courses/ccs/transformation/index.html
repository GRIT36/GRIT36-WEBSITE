Session 2 / Business model innovation

Business model transformation routes

The innovation matrix combines replication versus renewal with strategy-driven versus customer-driven change. Its four routes link the direction of change to leadership, managerial involvement, knowledge absorption and organizational identity.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

RouteCore changeSupporting conditions in the lecture
Explore and dominateStrategy-driven renewal: organization-wide transformation.Transformational leadership; top and middle management; innovative culture; internal knowledge absorption; a new internal identity.

Key distinctions

Direction and mode are separate choices

Customer-driven does not necessarily mean incremental, and strategy-driven does not necessarily mean radical. Either orientation can accompany replication or renewal. Use both axes when describing the transformation.

From a desired model to an organizational route

The matrix helps connect a proposed model with the way change is mobilized. It asks which managerial levels should be involved, where knowledge will come from and which identity or cultural characteristics might support the route.

How to use it

  1. Define whether the intended change is mainly replication or renewal.
  2. Clarify whether the direction is primarily strategy-driven or customer-driven.
  3. Check leadership, managerial commitment, knowledge absorption and identity against the chosen route.

Keep in mind

A firm can move between routes or combine them. The matrix does not prescribe a single universal sequence.

Replication, renewal & dual focus

Business model replication develops and extends an existing logic; business model renewal changes the model more substantially. The lecture distinguishes fixation, a focus on one route and dual focus on both routes.

Technology can enable new activities, but it is only one of the course's four levers. Management practices, organizational forms and co-creation affect whether an innovation can actually change how value is created and captured.
