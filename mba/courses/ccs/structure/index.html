Session 4 / Organization

Strategy–structure alignment

The multidivisional form consists of operating divisions with their own functional hierarchies. The course compares cooperative, strategic-business-unit and competitive variants, showing how coordination should reflect the relatedness of businesses.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

M-form variantStrategy association in the courseCoordination logic
Cooperative formRelated-constrained diversification.Divisions share important resources or activities and require significant interdivisional coordination.
Strategic business unit formRelated-linked diversification.Related divisions are grouped within SBUs, with a further corporate level coordinating the groups.
Competitive formUnrelated diversification.Businesses are more autonomous; corporate functions emphasize financial and administrative oversight.

Key distinctions

What alignment means

A structure distributes authority and determines which decisions are coordinated. Related businesses may benefit from shared planning or resources, but those arrangements create dependencies. Unrelated businesses may need autonomy while still being subject to performance monitoring and capital allocation.

Fit is a relationship

The lecture emphasizes that different structures support different competitive and corporate strategies. The test is not whether an organization chart appears modern; it is whether the chart, control systems and managerial responsibilities support the chosen activities and interdependencies.

How to use it

  1. Specify where cross-business interdependence creates value.
  2. Identify the decisions that must be coordinated and those best left local.
  3. Check whether structure, responsibilities and control systems support that division.

Keep in mind

The forms are simplified models. Real organizations may combine them, and a formal reporting line alone does not ensure cooperation.

Pause & recall

Why might an unrelated portfolio use a competitive M-form?

Reveal the explanation

Its businesses need less operational coordination and can be managed with greater autonomy and financial oversight.

Synergy & its hidden costs

Synergy exists when businesses achieve greater value together than they could independently. The course distinguishes the mechanisms creating that gain and the coordination costs, reduced responsiveness and managerial biases that can offset it.

Corporate parenting roles

A corporate parent is the organizational layer above the business units, not necessarily only the central headquarters. It must explain how its involvement improves the businesses rather than simply increasing administration.

Measuring diversification

The Wrigley/Rumelt classification uses revenue ratios to distinguish single, dominant, related and unrelated businesses. The lecture then separates related-constrained and related-linked portfolios by the strength of a common core.

Read the framework ↗S04 / Portfolio analysis

The BCG growth–share matrix

The BCG matrix compares relative market share with market growth. It provides a compact way to discuss the competitive position, cash generation and resource needs of businesses within a portfolio.

Portfolio matrices beyond BCG

Different matrices address different allocation questions. The course compares market growth and share with competitive position, life-cycle stage, market attractiveness and the corporate parent's ability to add value.
