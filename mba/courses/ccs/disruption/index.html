Session 2 / Competitive change

Hypercompetition & invisible S-curves

A business model can lose strategic strength before its financial results deteriorate. The course contrasts relatively stable competition with hypercompetition, where advantages are shorter-lived and renewal must occur more frequently.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

SignalWhat to inspectWhy it matters
Competitive position curveDevelopments at the edges of the market and organization.The basis of advantage may be weakening before reported revenue changes.
Distinctive skills and capabilities curveWhether the firm's capabilities and management perspectives are evolving.An unchanged capability base may no longer match the emerging environment.

Key distinctions

Compare the two clock speeds

The disruption test compares the rate of change in the industry with the rate of change in the firm. Long periods of incremental erosion call for a different rhythm than short periods of advantage punctuated by repeated disruption. Look beyond current competitors to new entrants, autonomous ventures and complementary outsiders.

Managerial responses in the lecture

The lecture proposes attention to peripheral signals, timely changes in the composition of top management and proactive talent management. These are ways of responding to the invisible curves; they are not substitutes for defining the specific business model changes required.

How to use it

  1. Identify the dominant model and how its advantage is eroding.
  2. Compare industry change with the firm's actual rate of adaptation.
  3. Record signals for competitive position, capabilities and talent.
  4. Decide which signals warrant investigation before financial decline becomes obvious.

Keep in mind

The curves are a conceptual warning system, not a precise forecasting equation. Do not infer a fixed life span for every advantage from an illustrative chart.

Pause & recall

Why can strong current results conceal a problem?

Reveal the explanation

Financial results may lag deterioration in position, capabilities or talent.
