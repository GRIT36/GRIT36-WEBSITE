Session 6 / Balancing competing demands

Organizational ambidexterity

Ambidexterity is the capacity to pursue exploration and exploitation together. The lecture compares structural separation with a context that lets individuals balance both, and introduces four ways of combining different organizational forms.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

ApproachHow the balance is createdImplication
Structural ambidexterityExploration and exploitation are assigned to separate units or groups.Senior management must coordinate the units and resolve competing demands.
Contextual ambidexterityPeople balance exploration and exploitation within their work, supported by the organizational context.The context must support judgment, initiative and cooperation.
OppositionDifferent forms coexist across organizations, such as a flexible orchestrator with planned partners.Coordination crosses organizational boundaries.
LocationDifferent forms occupy different organizational locations.A flexible unit may operate within a more rigid organization.
SynthesisFeatures of flexible and planned forms are combined.Design must make the combination workable rather than merely contradictory.
Temporal separationAn organization shifts emphasis across time.The ability to switch and retain learning becomes important.

Key distinctions

Separation does not remove the integration problem

Structurally separate units can protect exploration from short-term demands. They also create a need to decide where resources, knowledge and decisions should be shared. Contextual ambidexterity moves more of the balancing task into everyday work.

Choose the level at which the tension is handled

The lecture’s alternatives locate the tension across organizations, across units, within an organizational design or across time. Make that choice explicit before assigning people conflicting goals.

How to use it

  1. Specify the exploitation activities that must remain reliable.
  2. Specify what exploration needs protection or discretion.
  3. Choose the level of separation or integration.
  4. Create coordination mechanisms and criteria for resource allocation.

Keep in mind

Ambidexterity is not an instruction to divide time equally between two activities. The appropriate balance and design depend on the tasks, time horizons and organizational context.

Pause & recall

How do structural and contextual ambidexterity differ?

Reveal the explanation

Structural ambidexterity separates activities into units; contextual ambidexterity enables individuals to balance the demands within their work.

Strategic renewal: exploration & exploitation

Strategic renewal changes the organization’s existing path: its competences, assumptions, activities and relationship with the environment. The lecture contrasts selection pressures with managerial adaptation, and exploitation of existing strengths with exploration of new possibilities.

Organizational flexibility

Flexibility is a response to environmental demands, supported by management capabilities and organizational design. The lecture distinguishes rigid, planned, flexible and chaotic forms. More change is not automatically better: the required flexibility must be compared with the organization’s actual and potential flexibility.

Renewal paths & the transition dip

Renewal is a transition between organizational forms, not simply a decision to become more flexible. The lecture contrasts routinization with revitalization and illustrates the possibility of a temporary performance decline during transformation.

Four journeys of strategic renewal

The lecture distinguishes renewal journeys by the exploration or exploitation orientation of top management and of middle/front-line management. Each journey assigns a different role to initiative, integration and industry rules.

Transactional & transformational leadership

The lecture contrasts leadership based on exchanges, coordination and established expectations with leadership that mobilizes people around changed values, aspirations and direction. The comparison provides a starting point for considering how leadership supports strategic renewal.
