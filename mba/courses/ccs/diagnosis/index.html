Session 5 / Strategic diagnosis

SWOT as a synthesis of analysis

In the course sequence, SWOT is the result of internal and external analysis. Strengths and weaknesses concern the organization; opportunities and threats concern the environment. The useful work lies in explaining how these factors interact.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

QuadrantOrientationEvidence to bring
StrengthsInternal / positiveRelevant resources, capabilities or activities that support the focal objective.
WeaknessesInternal / negativeInternal limitations that obstruct the objective or response.
OpportunitiesExternal / positiveChanges or conditions that could support value creation.
ThreatsExternal / negativeChanges or conditions that could undermine it.

Key distinctions

The analysis feeding SWOT

Internal inputs include value-chain, resource/capability and activity-system analysis. External inputs include macro-environmental analysis, industry and product-market analysis, customers and, in the expanded version, ecosystems. This sequence discourages unsupported brainstorming of adjectives.

From overview to interaction

The lecture distinguishes a four-quadrant overview from analysis of relationships. Ask how a strength changes the ability to respond to an opportunity or threat, and how a weakness makes an external development more consequential.

The impact sheet

One source slide places internal strengths and weaknesses in rows and external changes in columns, with positive and negative impact scores. Its role is to make interactions explicit. A numerical score needs a stated rationale; adding scores does not remove uncertainty or judgment.

How to use it

  1. Gather and classify evidence at the appropriate analytical level.
  2. Select the factors material to the strategic question.
  3. Describe the most important interactions and their implications.
  4. Turn those implications into choices or further questions to investigate.

Keep in mind

Do not classify the same vague statement in several boxes or treat a scored worksheet as an objective forecast. Explain the perspective and the mechanism.

Pause & recall

Why does the course place SWOT after other analyses?

Reveal the explanation

Because it should synthesize grounded findings rather than replace the work needed to establish them.

S05 / Internal analysis

Value chain & activity systems

Value-chain analysis identifies the activities through which an organization transforms inputs into outputs. The activity-system view then asks how resources and capabilities connect across those activities to support a strategic position.

Read the framework ↗S05 / Internal analysis

The VRINO framework

VRINO examines whether a resource, routine, capability or competence can support sustained competitive advantage. The course uses five tests: Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, Non-substitutable and Organized.

Read the framework ↗S05 / External analysis

PESTEL macro-environmental analysis

PESTEL organizes changes in the wider environment into six categories. Its purpose in the course's analysis sequence is to identify relevant external developments before examining industry structure, competitors and customers.

Read the framework ↗S05 / Industry analysis

Industry structure, life cycle & strategic groups

The course separates three levels of industry analysis: industry characteristics, strategic groups and rivalry between specific competitors. These levels answer different questions and should not be collapsed into one competitor list.

Read the framework ↗S05 / Industry analysis

The four corners of competitor analysis

To reason about a competitor's future strategy, examine its drivers, management assumptions, current strategy and capabilities. The course places these four perspectives around the question of what the competitor is likely to do next.

Read the framework ↗S05 / Ecosystems

Ecosystems, complements & structure

An ecosystem links multiple actors through complementary contributions to a value proposition without placing them all under one hierarchy. The analysis asks how joint value comes about and which interdependencies must be aligned.

Read the framework ↗S05 / Alliances

Alliance forms & governance

Alliances coordinate activities across firm boundaries. The lecture distinguishes equity-based arrangements from contractual arrangements, alongside other associational forms, and explains how the form affects commitment and coordination.

Read the framework ↗S05 / Alliances

Partner selection, coopetition & alliance dynamics

The choice of a partner is only the beginning. Alliance performance depends on the fit of contributions and the continuing management of interdependence, uncertainty and changing objectives after formation.
