Session 6 / Roles & integration

Four journeys of strategic renewal

The lecture distinguishes renewal journeys by the exploration or exploitation orientation of top management and of middle/front-line management. Each journey assigns a different role to initiative, integration and industry rules.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

Renewal journeyManagement orientationKnowledge and integration logic
EmergentTop management: exploitation. Middle/front-line management: exploitation.The market knows best. Limited integration, a holding-company logic, and following industry rules.
DirectedTop management: exploration. Middle/front-line management: exploitation.Top management knows best. Hierarchical integration guides renewal and adapts industry rules.
FacilitatedTop management: exploitation. Middle/front-line management: exploration.Middle/front-line management knows best. Loosely connected initiatives are supported and can influence industry rules.
TransformationalTop management: exploration. Middle/front-line management: exploration.The organization knows best. High integration and tight connections support company-wide renewal and changing industry rules.

Key distinctions

A vocabulary for who initiates change

The matrix helps distinguish renewal led from the top from renewal that emerges through lower-level initiatives. The associated integration patterns explain why the same initiative can develop differently in different organizational settings.

Passive and active renewal

The lecture also contrasts a more passive response to environmental change with active managerial attempts to shape renewal. This is a question about agency and coordination, not simply whether a company announces a transformation program.

How to use it

  1. Identify where exploratory initiatives originate.
  2. Assess what top and middle/front-line management are actually doing.
  3. Map the integration mechanisms connecting initiatives.
  4. Check whether that pattern fits the scale and coordination needs of the renewal.

Keep in mind

These are ideal-type journeys, not a ranking from bad to good. A company can move between them, and units can exhibit different patterns at the same time.

Pause & recall

What separates directed from facilitated renewal?

Reveal the explanation

Directed renewal places exploration primarily at the top; facilitated renewal places it primarily in middle/front-line management.

S06 / Renewal foundations

Strategic renewal: exploration & exploitation

Strategic renewal changes the organization’s existing path: its competences, assumptions, activities and relationship with the environment. The lecture contrasts selection pressures with managerial adaptation, and exploitation of existing strengths with exploration of new possibilities.

Organizational flexibility

Flexibility is a response to environmental demands, supported by management capabilities and organizational design. The lecture distinguishes rigid, planned, flexible and chaotic forms. More change is not automatically better: the required flexibility must be compared with the organization’s actual and potential flexibility.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Changing the organization

Renewal paths & the transition dip

Renewal is a transition between organizational forms, not simply a decision to become more flexible. The lecture contrasts routinization with revitalization and illustrates the possibility of a temporary performance decline during transformation.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Balancing competing demands

Organizational ambidexterity

Ambidexterity is the capacity to pursue exploration and exploitation together. The lecture compares structural separation with a context that lets individuals balance both, and introduces four ways of combining different organizational forms.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Leading renewal

Transactional & transformational leadership

The lecture contrasts leadership based on exchanges, coordination and established expectations with leadership that mobilizes people around changed values, aspirations and direction. The comparison provides a starting point for considering how leadership supports strategic renewal.
