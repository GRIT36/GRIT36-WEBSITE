Session 6 / Changing the organization

Renewal paths & the transition dip

Renewal is a transition between organizational forms, not simply a decision to become more flexible. The lecture contrasts routinization with revitalization and illustrates the possibility of a temporary performance decline during transformation.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

IdeaWhat changesPractical question
RoutinizationActivities become more established and coordinated; flexibility can narrow as patterns become fixed.Which routines create reliability, and which now obstruct adaptation?
RevitalizationThe organization rebuilds its capacity to respond and renew.Which management capabilities and design features need to change together?
Transition dipPerformance may initially fall while old arrangements are disrupted and new ones are still developing.What evidence would distinguish a learning period from a failing transition?
Flexibility auditRequired, actual and potential flexibility are compared before selecting a change trajectory.Is the problem a lack of managerial repertoire, a lack of maneuverability, or both?

Key distinctions

Read the J-curve carefully

The lecture’s J-curve is a conceptual illustration of an initial decline followed by improvement. It is not a promise of eventual recovery. A transition needs milestones, feedback and a way to revise the approach if the expected capabilities do not develop.

Design the journey, not only the destination

A target organization may look coherent on paper while the transition creates contradictory demands. Use the flexibility audit to connect environmental needs with changes in capabilities, technology, structure and culture.

How to use it

  1. Establish the current organizational form and its strengths.
  2. Identify the specific gap that renewal should close.
  3. Sequence changes so that essential activities remain coordinated.
  4. Track learning, capability development and performance through the transition.

Keep in mind

The renewal paths and performance curve are conceptual tools. They do not predict the duration or size of a performance dip, and they do not justify continuing a failing change program indefinitely.

Pause & recall

What does the transition dip tell a manager to do?

Reveal the explanation

Plan for disruption and monitor whether new capabilities are actually emerging. A dip alone is not evidence that the transformation is working.

Strategic renewal: exploration & exploitation

Strategic renewal changes the organization’s existing path: its competences, assumptions, activities and relationship with the environment. The lecture contrasts selection pressures with managerial adaptation, and exploitation of existing strengths with exploration of new possibilities.

Organizational flexibility

Flexibility is a response to environmental demands, supported by management capabilities and organizational design. The lecture distinguishes rigid, planned, flexible and chaotic forms. More change is not automatically better: the required flexibility must be compared with the organization’s actual and potential flexibility.

Organizational ambidexterity

Ambidexterity is the capacity to pursue exploration and exploitation together. The lecture compares structural separation with a context that lets individuals balance both, and introduces four ways of combining different organizational forms.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Roles & integration

Four journeys of strategic renewal

The lecture distinguishes renewal journeys by the exploration or exploitation orientation of top management and of middle/front-line management. Each journey assigns a different role to initiative, integration and industry rules.

Transactional & transformational leadership

The lecture contrasts leadership based on exchanges, coordination and established expectations with leadership that mobilizes people around changed values, aspirations and direction. The comparison provides a starting point for considering how leadership supports strategic renewal.
