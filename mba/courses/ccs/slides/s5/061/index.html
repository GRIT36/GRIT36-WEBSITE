Choice of alliance partner
 An appropriate alliance partner contributes to the alliance’s intended goals without this being at the expense of the 
company’s other strategic goals.
 At least three different partner traits have a positive effect on alliance performance (Shah & Swaminathan, 2008):
 partner complementarity: the degree to which a partner can contribute valuable nonoverlapping resources and 
capabilities to the alliance (e.g., Dyer & Singh, 1998; Mowery et al., 1996) 
 partner compatibility: the fit between partners in terms of, for instance, corporate culture, type of resources, 
decision-making style and working practices (e.g., Mitsuhashi & Greve, 2017). 
 partner commitment: a partner’s willingness to contribute the resources required to achieve the alliance goals even 
when this requires making minor sacrifices (Gundlach et al., 1995).
 The criticality of these traits in comparison to each other can vary according to conditions such as partners’ size and 
longevity (Rothermael & Boeker, 2008)and the extent to which it is clear to them what exactly should be done 
during the lifetime of the alliance to achieve its long-term goals (Kale & Singh, 2009).
