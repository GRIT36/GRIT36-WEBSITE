Licensing agreements
Source: Volberda et al. (2023),
 A licensing agreement between a licensor (the company granting the license) and licensee implies that the latter party is 
authorized to use and earn revenue from certain intellectual property and/or other assets of the licensor in exchange for 
a negotiated payment.
 License payments often consist of an upfront lump-sum fee in conjunction with ongoing, usage-based fees called royalties 
(e.g., €0,50 for each unit sold, or 10% of revenues), but other terms are not uncommon.
 The licensor and licensee may agree on a certain cap on the amount of royalties payable under the life of the agreement 
or in any given specific period. They may also agree on certain scaling terms.
 Cross-licensing agreements (usually royalty-free) imply that two or more companies grant a license to each other, such as 
patents over different aspects of the same technology or product.
 Exclusive licensing agreement versus sole licensing agreement versus non-exclusive licensing agreement.
 Besides stipulating compensation and license exclusivity and duration, licensing agreements typically include provisions 
concerning the field of use of the licensed property (e.g., specifying the range of products that the licensee is allowed to 
sell utilizing this property and the geographical area of these sales), performance guarantees (e.g., quality standards and a 
minimal guaranteed use of the licensed property), license transferability and dispute settlement
