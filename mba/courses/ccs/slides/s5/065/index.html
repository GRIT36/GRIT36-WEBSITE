Characteristics of interorganizational collaborations that can change over time 
Source: Majchrzak et al. (2015) 64
1. Goal or mission of the collaboration: add new goal, change priority ranking of existing goals, 
or modify, replace or drop a certain goal.
2. Organizational structure: extent of formalization and standardization of roles, procedures and 
processes of interorganizational collaboration.
3. Contract frame: transactional versus relational elements in the interorganizational 
agreements on which collaborating firms rely in their interactions.
4. Interaction style: from competitive to cooperative (higher degree of open information sharing, 
without hidden agendas) and vice versa on a continuum scale.
5. Decision-making control: the locus of interorganizational decision making.
6. Actor composition: adding of new actors (i.e., companies, other organizations or individuals) 
or the exclusion or replacement of existing ones.
