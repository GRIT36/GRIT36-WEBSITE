The reading path

6 frameworks & concepts

S06 / Renewal foundations

Strategic renewal: exploration & exploitation

Strategic renewal changes the organization’s existing path: its competences, assumptions, activities and relationship with the environment. The lecture contrasts selection pressures with managerial adaptation, and exploitation of existing strengths with exploration of new possibilities.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Environmental fit

Organizational flexibility

Flexibility is a response to environmental demands, supported by management capabilities and organizational design. The lecture distinguishes rigid, planned, flexible and chaotic forms. More change is not automatically better: the required flexibility must be compared with the organization’s actual and potential flexibility.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Changing the organization

Renewal paths & the transition dip

Renewal is a transition between organizational forms, not simply a decision to become more flexible. The lecture contrasts routinization with revitalization and illustrates the possibility of a temporary performance decline during transformation.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Balancing competing demands

Organizational ambidexterity

Ambidexterity is the capacity to pursue exploration and exploitation together. The lecture compares structural separation with a context that lets individuals balance both, and introduces four ways of combining different organizational forms.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Roles & integration

Four journeys of strategic renewal

The lecture distinguishes renewal journeys by the exploration or exploitation orientation of top management and of middle/front-line management. Each journey assigns a different role to initiative, integration and industry rules.

Read the framework ↗S06 / Leading renewal

Transactional & transformational leadership

The lecture contrasts leadership based on exchanges, coordination and established expectations with leadership that mobilizes people around changed values, aspirations and direction. The comparison provides a starting point for considering how leadership supports strategic renewal.
