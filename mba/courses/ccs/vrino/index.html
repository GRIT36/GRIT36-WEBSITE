The VRINO framework

VRINO examines whether a resource, routine, capability or competence can support sustained competitive advantage. The course uses five tests: Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, Non-substitutable and Organized.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

TestQuestionIf the test is not met
V — ValuableDoes it contribute to a relevant value-creation opportunity or response?Possession alone does not support a competitive advantage.
R — RareIs it uncommon among the relevant competitors?It may support parity rather than a distinctive position.
I — InimitableIs it difficult for competitors to copy?Any distinctive benefit may be vulnerable to imitation.
N — Non-substitutableCan a different resource or approach provide an equivalent benefit?The advantage may be vulnerable to alternative solutions.
O — OrganizedIs the firm organized to exploit it?Potential value can remain unused.

Key distinctions

Resources and deployment

The first four tests concern the characteristics of what the firm possesses or can do. The final test asks whether organizational arrangements enable it to use that potential. A valuable asset with no supporting processes, authority or complementary activities may fail to deliver results.

Use the course's version

The separate N test is part of the VRINO version used here. Keep it visible rather than silently replacing the framework with a shorter acronym encountered elsewhere.

How to use it

  1. Choose a specific resource or capability rather than an entire company.
  2. Provide evidence for each of the five tests.
  3. Identify whether the limiting issue is the asset itself or the organization that deploys it.

Keep in mind

The assessment is relative to a competitive context and time period. Do not interpret 'inimitable' as a promise that a position can never be challenged.

Pause & recall

Why is the O test necessary?

Reveal the explanation

Because potential advantage does not automatically translate into results without appropriate organization.

The four corners of competitor analysis

To reason about a competitor's future strategy, examine its drivers, management assumptions, current strategy and capabilities. The course places these four perspectives around the question of what the competitor is likely to do next.

SWOT as a synthesis of analysis

In the course sequence, SWOT is the result of internal and external analysis. Strengths and weaknesses concern the organization; opportunities and threats concern the environment. The useful work lies in explaining how these factors interact.
