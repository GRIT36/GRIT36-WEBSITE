Session 6 / Environmental fit

Organizational flexibility

Flexibility is a response to environmental demands, supported by management capabilities and organizational design. The lecture distinguishes rigid, planned, flexible and chaotic forms. More change is not automatically better: the required flexibility must be compared with the organization’s actual and potential flexibility.

ENGLISH STUDY GUIDE Edited explanation based on the course. Original wording and figures appear below.

The framework

Organizational formEnvironment and responseCapabilities and design
RigidStable, relatively simple and predictable conditions. Strategic programming and routines dominate.Limited management repertoire; low maneuverability; mechanistic structure and conservative culture.
PlannedMore dynamic and complex conditions that remain sufficiently predictable. Planning coordinates a response.Operational flexibility; a moderate management repertoire and maneuverability.
FlexibleHypercompetition: dynamic, complex and unpredictable conditions. Adaptation and learning become central.Operational, structural and strategic flexibility; broad repertoire and high maneuverability; organic, innovative design.
ChaoticExtremely turbulent conditions. Ad hoc problem solving and spontaneous responses dominate.Broad repertoire but low maneuverability in the lecture’s summary matrix. A surplus of strategic flexibility need not produce coordinated action.

Key distinctions

Actual versus potential flexibility

Actual flexibility concerns management capabilities: operational flexibility changes the volume or mix of activities; structural flexibility changes how activities are organized; strategic flexibility changes the organization’s direction. Potential flexibility concerns maneuverability created by technology, structure and culture.

Run a flexibility audit

The lecture compares required flexibility, derived from environmental scanning, with actual management capabilities and potential maneuverability. Gaps inform change trajectories, followed by attention to people, resources, time and evaluation.

How to use it

  1. Describe the environment’s dynamism, complexity and predictability.
  2. Identify the required operational, structural and strategic responses.
  3. Assess the organization’s current repertoire and maneuverability.
  4. Specify a change trajectory with resources, timing and evaluation criteria.

Keep in mind

The four forms are ideal types. An organization may contain different forms across units, and the same form can be appropriate in one environment and unsuitable in another.

Pause & recall

Why is a broad management repertoire not enough?

Reveal the explanation

Managers also need organizational maneuverability. Technology, structure and culture can prevent intended responses from being implemented.

Organizational ambidexterity

Ambidexterity is the capacity to pursue exploration and exploitation together. The lecture compares structural separation with a context that lets individuals balance both, and introduces four ways of combining different organizational forms.

Four journeys of strategic renewal

The lecture distinguishes renewal journeys by the exploration or exploitation orientation of top management and of middle/front-line management. Each journey assigns a different role to initiative, integration and industry rules.

Transactional & transformational leadership

The lecture contrasts leadership based on exchanges, coordination and established expectations with leadership that mobilizes people around changed values, aspirations and direction. The comparison provides a starting point for considering how leadership supports strategic renewal.
